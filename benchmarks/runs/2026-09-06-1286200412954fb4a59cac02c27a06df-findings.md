## What was run

The stage-1 crossover: a hand-written CUDA C++ kernel evaluating Ramanujan's
1914 series against Quantum Amplitude Estimation simulated on cuStateVec, on
one RTX 5070 Laptop GPU. Schema 4, protocol digest `24d1d3af702a`, clean source
revision `15c0da6`, CUDA-Q target `nvidia` at fp32, driver 616.56, power profile
`turbo`, 4000 shots, 5 timed repeats per configuration after one discarded
warm-up, 27 rows.

This is the first run collected under the measurement protocol committed in
`benchmarks/protocol.py`, whose declaration and digest travel in this file's
`protocol` block. That block records the stopping rule — the sweep and repeat
count are fixed in advance and not extended, truncated or re-run on the basis
of observed values — and the exclusion rule, which is that nothing is excluded.
A run file can show which rules were declared; it cannot by itself prove they
were followed. That compliance is attested by the reviewer, not by the data.

## The classical arm reproduces the earlier archive

Across all 12 term counts the mean wall time falls within ±12% of the
2026-08-05 archive, measured under the same power profile and shot count. The
largest configurations agree most closely: 16384 terms moved from 68.997 ms to
67.584 ms, and 4096 terms from 13.685 ms to 13.349 ms. Best accuracy is
unchanged at 16.00 correct digits, which is double precision's ceiling rather
than a property of the series.

These timings are end-to-end and must not be read as device execution cost.
This file's `configuration.phase_attribution` records that the sweep measured
end-to-end only, and `configuration.timing_boundary` records that the timed
region is the `partial_sum` wrapper. The wrapper encloses kernel-handle
acquisition, device allocation, launch, the device-to-host copy and a host
reduction, so at low term counts — where every configuration from 1 to 256
terms sits within a narrow 2.4-3.3 ms band despite a 256-fold difference in
arithmetic — the measurement is plainly not dominated by arithmetic. This
archive does not apportion that cost between phases; doing so requires a
phase-attributed run, which this is not.

## QAE timing moved with register size, and the direction reversed

Relative to the earlier archive, small counting registers are faster and the
two largest are slower. At m = 2 the ratio is 0.72 and it trends upward to 1.10
at m = 15 and 1.07 at m = 16. The trend is not monotonic: five of the fourteen
steps move downward (at m = 5, 6, 9, 14 and 16), so the shape is a broad drift
rather than a clean progression. Every ratio from m = 2 to m = 14 is below 1.0
and both m = 15 and m = 16 are above it, so the crossing lies between m = 14
and m = 15.

No explanation for this pattern is offered, because this run cannot support
one. The two archives differ in at least driver version (610.88 to 616.56) and
in date, neither of which was varied deliberately, and the sweep ran for 2m18s,
over which laptop thermal drift is neither controlled nor recorded per
configuration. Any account of the reversal — driver, thermal, or otherwise —
requires an experiment that varies one factor at a time. This is recorded as an
observation awaiting that experiment.

## Every quantum outcome reproduced exactly

All 15 QAE configurations return the same pi estimate as the 2026-08-05
archive to within 1e-12, and every one agrees with the closed-form prediction
in `quantum/quantization.py`. Each row's sampled outcome distribution sits
below its own sampling tolerance.

This is the expected result rather than a surprise: the estimate is quantized
onto a grid of 2^m reachable amplitudes, so shot noise decides only how
reliably the correct grid point wins, not which one it is. Its value here is
that it makes the timing comparison meaningful — the two runs demonstrably
computed the same thing, so the timing differences above are not confounded by
the arms having done different work.

Best QAE accuracy is 5.00 correct digits. That ceiling is the distance from the
true phase to the 2^m grid, not a limit of the simulator or of shot count.

## Limitations

This measures the cost of simulating a QAE circuit on a GPU; it is not evidence
about quantum hardware. The circuit's target amplitude is constructed to equal
pi/4, so recovering pi from the outcome already uses pi — this is a
resource-cost comparison, not two algorithms independently discovering pi.

Both arms ran on a single laptop GPU under one power profile; nothing here
describes other silicon. The comparison against the 2026-08-05 archive holds
power profile and shot count fixed but does not control driver version, ambient
or die temperature, or machine state, so it is a paired observation rather than
a controlled experiment. Timings are end-to-end and are not apportioned between
wrapper, transfer and device execution. The confidence intervals cover repeated
timings on this host and are a lower bound on real uncertainty, since timing
samples are not independent across thermal drift.
